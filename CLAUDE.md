# benjamincastleman.com — Personal Academic Website

## Outcome
A clean, fast, static personal academic website for Ben Castleman, live at **benjamincastleman.com** via GitHub Pages, that Ben can update by editing one data file and running one command.

Done means:
- Five nav items work: About (homepage), Research, Partnerships, Public Writing, CV (opens PDF)
- All content below appears exactly as written, with every link working
- Looks good on phone and desktop, in light mode, with no horizontal scrolling
- Adding a new paper = add one entry to a data file + run `python build.py`
- A short README explains how to update content and redeploy

## Design reference
Model the look on https://andrewrsimon.com (a colleague's site). Key traits:
- Header: name "Ben Castleman" on the left with a three-line subtitle beneath: "Professor of Public Policy and Education" / "Faculty Director of Research and Engagement" / "University of Virginia"; plain text nav links on the right (collapse gracefully on mobile — no hamburger unless truly needed)
- Homepage: headshot on the left, bio on the right (stacked on mobile)
- Light gray background (#f5f5f5, matching andrewrsimon.com), generous whitespace, one readable serif or clean sans-serif body font at a comfortable size (~17–18px), max content width ~720–800px
- No hero images, no animations, no cookie banners, no tracking
- Minimal footer: "© 2026 Ben Castleman"

## Technical constraints
- Static HTML/CSS output, no JavaScript framework. No JS at all unless needed for the mobile nav.
- Build: a single `build.py` (Python 3, only dependency PyYAML) that reads content from `content/*.yaml` and writes the finished HTML. Keep templates simple and readable.
- Serve from the repo root or `/docs` on GitHub Pages — your call; document it in the README.
- Include a `CNAME` file containing `benjamincastleman.com`.
- Basic SEO: page titles ("Research | Ben Castleman" etc.), meta description "Ben Castleman, Professor of Public Policy and Education at the University of Virginia", Open Graph tags, a simple favicon.
- Accessible: semantic HTML, alt text on the headshot, sufficient contrast.

### About (homepage)

I am a professor of public policy and education and faculty director of research and engagement at the University of Virginia's Frank Batten School of Leadership and Public Policy. My research focuses on policies and strategies to improve postsecondary educational and workforce outcomes for individuals from lower-income communities. I conduct this research through collaborative research-policy partnerships with public agencies and organizations at the local, state, and federal levels. My current work focuses on innovations to increase economic mobility among lower-wage adults and on the economic and life outcomes generated by intensive college advising programs.

My research has appeared in top journals, including the *American Economic Review*, the *Journal of Labor Economics*, the *Journal of Public Economics*, and the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*. I am a recipient of the Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers. My work has been generously supported by numerous philanthropic foundations and has received extensive media coverage, including in *The New York Times*, National Public Radio, and *The Washington Post*.

Before returning to graduate school, I was a public school teacher and administrator in Providence, RI.

### Partnerships

I conduct my research in close collaboration with public agencies, colleges and universities, and nonprofit organizations. Current partners include:

**State systems and public colleges and universities.** My work with the Virginia Community College System, the Tennessee Board of Regents, Calbright College, and the University of Hawaiʻi Community Colleges focuses on identifying the policies and supports that help students earn credentials and advance economically.

**College advising organizations.** With Bottom Line, College Possible, Matriculate, and CollegePoint, I evaluate how intensive advising shapes degree attainment and, over the long run, earnings and broader life outcomes.

**Workforce training programs.** I study innovative training models, including Virginia FastForward, Merit America, and Coop Careers, to identify what works to improve economic outcomes for working adults.
